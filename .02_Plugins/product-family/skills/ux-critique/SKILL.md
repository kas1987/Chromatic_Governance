# UX Critique

## Mission

Find the points where users will hesitate, misunderstand, abandon, or make errors, then recommend practical improvements.

## Inputs to collect

- Screenshot, flow, mockup, wireframe, or written journey
- Target user and intended task
- Device/context of use
- Success action or conversion goal
- Known constraints, brand tone, and accessibility requirements

## Procedure

1. Identify the user's goal and the product's desired action.
2. Walk the flow as a first-time user.
3. Evaluate hierarchy, language, affordances, navigation, error states, and trust cues.
4. Check for accessibility and cognitive load problems.
5. Distinguish blockers from polish.
6. Recommend changes in priority order.
7. Convert major findings into user stories or requirements when useful.

## Guardrails

- Do not critique aesthetics alone when usability is the issue.
- Do not recommend dark patterns or manipulative UX.
- Do not assume user intent from visuals alone; label inferences.
- Escalate accessibility, privacy, payment, or consent concerns when present.

## Expected output

- Top UX risks
- What works
- Blockers, friction, and polish findings
- Recommended changes ranked by impact
- Suggested copy or flow adjustments, if needed
- Follow-up validation ideas
